# German A1 Study Companion — Product Design

## Purpose

A personal study companion for a German A1 learner at Annemarie-Schimmel-Haus (ASH).  
It helps the learner stay organised, revise consistently, understand weaknesses, and prepare for internal tests and the Goethe A1 exam.

The app is **not a replacement for the course, teacher, textbook, or Allango**. It is a helper and progress tracker that turns course material and daily study into a clear, repeatable routine.

## Target user

A beginner German learner who:

- Is enrolled in an intensive A1 course at ASH.
- Uses *Netzwerk neu A1* and the Allango digital platform.
- Wants to achieve a high internal A1 result, ideally 85% or above.
- Needs a structured system for vocabulary, grammar, listening, speaking, writing, and revision.
- Wants to continue to A2 and eventually prepare for Goethe A2/B1 exams.

## Core promise

> “I always know what to revise, what I am weak at, and what I should practise today.”

The product should make progress visible and make revision automatic, without overwhelming the learner with too much content.

## What the app does

The app helps the learner:

- Organise course material by chapter, topic, page, audio, video, and exercise.
- Track vocabulary, grammar rules, mistakes, homework, and study sessions.
- Review material through spaced repetition.
- Practise producing German through writing and speaking.
- Identify recurring weaknesses and prioritise them.
- Prepare for internal ASH tests and the Goethe A1 exam.
- Receive reminders and daily study plans.
- Access official Allango material through authorised links or permitted downloads.

## What the app does not do

The app does not:

- Replace the ASH teacher, classroom, textbook, or Allango platform.
- Teach German as a standalone course.
- Host or redistribute copyrighted textbook PDFs, audio, or videos without permission.
- Automatically collect or analyse the institute WhatsApp group without consent.
- Act as an authority for exam dates, admission rules, or official course requirements.
- Give unsupported grammar corrections or invented explanations.

## Product principles

1. **Official material stays central.**  
   Allango and *Netzwerk neu A1* remain the primary sources of lessons, audio, videos, exercises, and answers.

2. **The learner’s mistakes become the curriculum.**  
   The app should focus revision on the words, grammar patterns, and skills the learner actually gets wrong.

3. **Recognition is not mastery.**  
   A word is mastered only when the learner can use it correctly in a new sentence after time has passed.

4. **Small daily actions beat large occasional study sessions.**  
   The app should encourage short, consistent sessions rather than cramming.

5. **Every recommendation must be explainable.**  
   The learner should always understand why something is being recommended.

6. **Corrections must be trustworthy.**  
   Grammar suggestions should be linked to a lesson, rule, example, or verified source whenever possible.

## Main user journey

1. The learner attends class and studies the current *Netzwerk* chapter.
2. The learner adds key vocabulary, grammar rules, examples, and mistakes to the app.
3. The app schedules revision based on what has been learned and forgotten.
4. The learner completes a short daily session: review, listening, speaking, writing, and grammar practice.
5. The app identifies weak areas and adjusts future practice.
6. Before an internal test, the app shows readiness, gaps, and a focused revision plan.
7. After the test, the learner records the result and updates weak areas.

## Key features

### Course map

A visual map of the A1 course, organised by chapter.

Each chapter should show:

- Chapter title and main topics.
- Links to the relevant Allango book pages, audio, videos, and exercises.
- Vocabulary learned and still pending.
- Grammar rules covered.
- Workbook exercises completed.
- Internal-test status.
- Overall chapter mastery.

The learner should immediately see: “What have I completed, what is pending, and what needs revision?”

### Daily study plan

Each day, the app shows a small, prioritised plan:

- Review due vocabulary.
- Practise one weak grammar pattern.
- Listen to one relevant dialogue or audio.
- Speak aloud for a few minutes.
- Write 5–10 sentences.
- Complete any pending homework or workbook exercise.
- Prepare for an approaching test.

The plan should be short enough to complete consistently, ideally around 30–60 minutes outside class.

### Vocabulary mastery

Vocabulary should never be stored as a simple word-and-translation pair.

For nouns, the app should capture:

- German word.
- Article: der, die, or das.
- Plural form.
- English/Urdu meaning.
- Example sentence.
- Personal sentence.
- Audio pronunciation.
- Chapter and source.
- Related grammar pattern.

For verbs, it should capture:

- Infinitive.
- Conjugation.
- Common sentence pattern.
- Example sentence.
- Personal sentence.
- Related grammar rule.

A word should move through stages such as:

- New
- Learning
- Familiar
- Usable
- Mastered

A word reaches “mastered” only after successful recall and correct use in a fresh sentence across multiple review sessions.

### Grammar tracker

The app should track grammar as practical patterns, not abstract rules.

Examples:

- Verb-second word order in statements.
- Verb-first word order in yes/no questions.
- Noun gender and plural forms.
- Nominative and accusative cases.
- Present-tense verb conjugation.
- Common prepositions and contractions.
- Separable verbs.
- Modal verbs.

Each grammar rule should include:

- A simple explanation.
- Examples from the course.
- Common mistakes.
- Practice sentences.
- Links to the relevant chapter and Allango material.
- The learner’s accuracy and recent mistakes.

### Mistake log

Every mistake is valuable data.

When the learner makes an error, the app should record:

- The incorrect sentence or answer.
- The corrected version.
- The mistake category.
- The grammar rule involved.
- The related vocabulary.
- The chapter or lesson.
- A short explanation.
- Whether the mistake has been repeated.

The app should group mistakes into patterns, such as:

- Article errors.
- Verb-conjugation errors.
- Word-order errors.
- Case errors.
- Preposition errors.
- Spelling errors.
- Listening-comprehension gaps.
- Speaking hesitation.

The dashboard should show the learner’s biggest weaknesses, not just a list of past mistakes.

### Sentence coach

When the learner writes a German sentence, the app should help them improve it without simply giving a vague correction.

For each sentence, it should show:

- The corrected sentence.
- The type of mistake.
- The grammar rule involved.
- A short explanation.
- Related examples.
- Links to the relevant chapter or vocabulary.
- A few similar practice sentences.

Example:

> Learner writes: “Ich habe ein Tisch gekauft.”  
> App responds: “Tisch is masculine. In this sentence, it needs the accusative form: ‘Ich habe einen Tisch gekauft.’ Review: accusative case and noun gender.”

The app should distinguish between:

- A confirmed correction based on a known A1 rule.
- A likely suggestion that needs learner review.
- An unsupported or uncertain response.

### Listening practice

The app should help the learner use course audio actively rather than passively.

A listening session may include:

- Listen once without text.
- Listen again while reading.
- Identify key words.
- Repeat sentences aloud.
- Answer simple comprehension questions.
- Shadow the speaker.
- Record a personal version of the dialogue.

The app should track which audio resources have been completed and which words or structures caused difficulty.

### Speaking practice

The app should encourage speaking from the beginning.

Daily speaking tasks could include:

- Introduce yourself.
- Describe your family.
- Talk about your daily routine.
- Describe a picture.
- Answer likely A1 questions.
- Retell a short dialogue.
- Record a 30–60 second voice note.

The app should store the recording, allow self-rating, and highlight repeated hesitation or vocabulary gaps. It should not pretend to replace a human examiner.

### Writing practice

The app should give short, achievable writing tasks:

- Write five sentences about your day.
- Describe your family.
- Write a message to a friend.
- Describe your home.
- Write about your hobbies.
- Answer a common A1 exam prompt.

After writing, the learner should receive:

- Corrections.
- Grammar explanations.
- Vocabulary suggestions.
- A record of recurring mistakes.
- A revised version to practise again.

### Test preparation

The app should support both internal ASH assessments and the Goethe A1 exam.

It should include:

- Test-date countdown.
- Syllabus coverage by chapter.
- Practice-test results.
- Weak-area revision plan.
- Vocabulary readiness.
- Grammar readiness.
- Listening, reading, writing, and speaking practice.
- A realistic readiness estimate.

The learner should be able to see whether they are prepared for the whole A1 syllabus or only for recent chapters.

### WhatsApp capture

The app should help the learner capture important information from the institute WhatsApp group without automatically monitoring private conversations.

The learner can:

- Forward or paste an important message.
- Upload a screenshot.
- Extract dates, deadlines, homework, announcements, and links.
- Turn the information into tasks and reminders.
- Review and approve the extracted information before saving it.

The app may later send private reminders to the learner through WhatsApp, but it should not send messages to classmates or the group without explicit permission.

## Progress dashboard

The dashboard should answer four questions:

1. **Am I on track?**
2. **What should I do today?**
3. **Where am I weak?**
4. **Am I ready for the next test?**

It should show:

- Study streak.
- Cards due today.
- Vocabulary mastered.
- Chapter completion.
- Grammar accuracy.
- Recent mistakes.
- Listening and speaking practice.
- Test countdown.
- Readiness estimate.
- Priority weak areas.

The dashboard should prioritise action over vanity metrics. A large vocabulary count is less useful than knowing which 20 words or grammar patterns are still causing errors.

## Progress model

Progress should be based on evidence, not feelings.

Track:

- Vocabulary recall success.
- Grammar accuracy.
- Workbook completion.
- Listening completion.
- Speaking frequency.
- Writing frequency.
- Mistake reduction over time.
- Internal-test scores.
- Practice-test scores.

The app should show progress in simple terms:

- “You have mastered 72% of Chapter 3 vocabulary.”
- “Your article accuracy has improved from 61% to 78%.”
- “Your weakest area is dative prepositions.”
- “You are not yet ready for the final test; focus on Chapters 4–6.”

## Review system

The review system should use spaced repetition.

A learner should see a word again:

- Soon after first learning it.
- After a successful recall.
- After a longer interval.
- Immediately after a mistake or low-confidence recall.

The system should also include:

- Listening-based cards.
- Sentence-production cards.
- Grammar-pattern cards.
- Error-retry cards.
- Chapter-based revision sets.

The goal is not to review everything equally. The goal is to review what is most likely to be forgotten or misused.

## Content sources

The app should clearly distinguish between sources:

| Source | Role |
|---|---|
| ASH class | Primary instruction, teacher feedback, and course expectations |
| *Netzwerk neu A1* | Primary textbook, exercises, vocabulary, and structure |
| Allango | Official digital material, audio, video, exercises, and offline access |
| Goethe practice material | Exam-style preparation |
| Learner notes | Personal examples, mistakes, and revision priorities |
| WhatsApp messages | Administrative information, deadlines, and homework reminders |
| AI assistance | Optional support for explanations, practice generation, and sentence feedback |

## Trust and accuracy

The app should avoid presenting uncertain AI output as fact.

Every grammar correction should ideally include:

- The corrected sentence.
- The reason for the correction.
- The grammar rule.
- A source or related lesson.
- An option for the learner to accept, reject, or flag the correction.

If the app cannot verify a correction confidently, it should say:

> “This may be incorrect. Please verify with your teacher or course material.”

This protects the learner from learning incorrect German.

## Privacy and boundaries

The app should be private by default.

- Study data belongs to the learner.
- WhatsApp content should only be added intentionally.
- Group messages should not be automatically collected or shared.
- Classmate information should not be stored or analysed.
- Audio recordings should be private unless the learner chooses to share them.
- Official copyrighted material should remain within the permissions of Allango and the publisher.

## Success criteria

The product is successful if the learner can honestly say:

- “I know what to study today.”
- “I understand why I made a mistake.”
- “I can see my progress clearly.”
- “I am revising the right material at the right time.”
- “I feel prepared before tests.”
- “I am using German actively, not only recognising it.”
- “I can continue to A2 without losing momentum.”

## First version focus

The first version should focus on:

1. Course map and chapter tracking.
2. Vocabulary capture with articles, plurals, examples, and audio links.
3. Daily review queue.
4. Mistake log.
5. Grammar-pattern tracker.
6. Basic sentence-writing feedback.
7. Test countdown and readiness overview.
8. WhatsApp message capture and reminders.

Later versions can add deeper AI feedback, speech analysis, group integrations, advanced analytics, and multi-level support for A2 and B1.
